# Brand Style Guide: Life3Baje

## Essence (read first)
**Life3Baje -- Building a Life Beyond the 9-to-5.** A personal documentary channel with vlogs inside it, not a daily vlog. It follows the creator as he tries to build a more interesting, independent and creative life while working a full-time software job: building YouTube channels, experimenting with businesses, learning violin, writing, road trips, buying and building things. He has not figured it out; the journey is the content. His channels, businesses, hobbies, purchases and trips are chapters of one story.
- **Never** generic productivity, motivation, business advice or YouTube-growth content. Never a list of ambitions read to camera: show the evidence (his real desk, timeline, notebook, dashboard, car, violin).
- **Tone:** honest, warm, conversational, first person; real numbers and real setbacks. No fake emotion for the camera; an ordinary moment is recorded as ordinary.
- **Language:** natural Hinglish in Roman script.

## The five pillars
1. **Building my YouTube channels** -- the main ongoing storyline: running several channels, production, new formats, failures, revenue and expenses, demonetisation, lessons from real results. ("I Am Trying to Run 4 YouTube Channels While Working Full-Time")
2. **Learning new things** -- violin from zero, poetry and stories, maybe a book; documented progress. ("I Started Learning Violin at 33. Here's My First Month.")
3. **Trips and experiences** -- solo road trips, sleeping in the car, quiet mountain towns, places he might live. ("I Drove Across India to Find a Place Where I Could Start Over.")
4. **Things I buy, build and try** -- purchases become stories, not reviews: creator gear, a sleeping setup inside his Duster, tools and gadgets tested in his own life. ("I Tried Turning My Old Car Into a Tiny Camper.")
5. **My creative life and personal experiments** -- the connective tissue: making time for writing, music and filmmaking; career and future decisions; honest reflections on what works and what does not. ("I Have Too Many Ideas and Not Enough Time to Finish Them.")

A video does not have to touch every pillar: a violin video can be all violin, a trip video all trip. Consistency comes from his personality, his perspective and the ongoing journey.

## Vlog versus story (the rule for every idea)
Every video needs a reason to exist beyond "what I did": a question, a challenge, a decision or an outcome nobody knows yet.
| Ordinary vlog | Life3Baje video |
|---|---|
| I bought a new camera | I Upgraded My Setup to Make Better Videos |
| My weekend road trip | I Escaped My Routine for 48 Hours |
| I practised violin | Can I Learn to Play One Song in 30 Days? |
| I worked on YouTube | I Tried Running Four Channels for 30 Days |
| I bought camping equipment | I Tried Building a Bed Inside My Old Duster |
Casual moments, conversations and travel footage support the story; they never replace it.

## Launch order (first six long videos)
1. I'm Trying to Build Something Beyond My Software Job -- the larger journey and why he documents it.
2. The Reality of Running Multiple YouTube Channels -- workflow, time limits, costs, real challenges.
3. I'm 33 and Starting to Learn Violin From Zero -- a challenge with visible progress over time.
4. Can I Turn My Old Duster Into a Mini Camper? -- a practical build with a clear goal and outcome.
5. I Want to Write, Create Videos and Learn Music. Why Am I Doing Everything? -- reflective, grounded in real events.
6. I Spent 30 Days Trying to Change My Life -- the progress report tying the channel, hobbies and goals together.

## For every video idea develop
Core premise and the open question; title options; thumbnail text; the cold-open tension (a real problem, never a logo or a greeting first); scene-by-scene script with direct-to-camera lines and voice-over; camera position and framing per shot; the real footage to film (his own -- desk, screen recordings, notebook, activity); natural sound and silence; editing notes; a measurable promise when there is a challenge (e.g. a "Next 30 days" page he keeps for the follow-up video); the link to the previous and next episodes. Fill-in gaps for real details are marked in [brackets] -- never invented numbers, results or milestones.

## Pictures and graphics
His own footage is the main picture: never generic motivational stock. **AI pictures support his ideas** -- a concept, a plan, a feeling, a comparison, a future he imagines -- where none of his clips fits (about a third of the picture time at most). They never stand in for his real life: no generated desk, room, screen, dashboard, notebook, car, violin, face or people presented as real. Graphics are sparse and clean, used where they help the viewer read the moment:
- **statement_card** / **kinetic_text** for the episode title card and the few lines meant to be read ("REAL PROJECTS. REAL PROGRESS. REAL SETBACKS.", "DAY 1 -> DAY 30").
- **pill_labels** / **icon_list** when he lists what he wants to build or learn; **numbered_point** for his three 30-day targets.
- **flow_diagram** for a process he describes (idea -> script -> recording -> editing -> publishing).
- **timeline** for an age, a date range or a before/after; **canvas_card** to show a dashboard, notebook page or purchase while keeping him on screen; **callout** to point at a real number on his own dashboard.
- **name_title** once, when he introduces himself. Private details (accounts, company code, revenue he does not want public) are blurred or left out of frame.

## The face in the long video
Two layouts, chosen per channel or per project: **mixed** (his face comes back between the pictures all through) or **face first** (only his face for the first 1-2 minutes, then pictures, screen recordings and clips only). Life3Baje is usually mixed: direct-to-camera lines alternate with observed footage of him working.

## Continuity
Generate new topics from what he is actually building and what happened since the previous episodes (projects in the Studio, channel results, purchases, trips, practice logs). Treat Life3Baje as one ongoing documentary; promises made on camera (30-day targets) come back as follow-up episodes.

## Music
Warm, understated instrumental beds under an honest first-person story -- never trailer-epic, never hype. Openings and everyday building: lo-fi or acoustic, soft piano, acoustic guitar, bansuri, calm/hopeful, 80-95 bpm. Wins and real progress: hopeful, piano with light drums, 100-115 bpm. Failures and hard numbers: sad solo violin or soft piano, 65-75 bpm, or `off`. The first seconds of a cold open are often silence plus natural sound. Indian colour (bansuri, santoor, tabla) is welcome; keep it gentle.

## Shorts
Made on the Shorts tab: honest moments cut from the long videos, and his recitations of Hindi poems and sher-o-shayari (recited verbatim, the poet always credited).
