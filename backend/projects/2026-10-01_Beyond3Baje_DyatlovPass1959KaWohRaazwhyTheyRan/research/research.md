# Research package

## Timeline

- Late 1958: The group of nine hikers, primarily students and graduates from Yekaterinburg, begins preparation and training for their winter trek.
- January 1959: The group travels by train to the Ural Mountains to begin their expedition.
- February 1, 1959: The hikers reach the area near Kholat Syakhl (Mountain of the Dead) and establish a base camp.
- February 2, 1959: The group sets up their tent at the final location; they are documented as having prepared for extreme conditions.
- Late Night February 2 / Early February 3, 1959: The 'incident' occurs where the hikers flee the tent in a state of distress or panic.
- February 23, 1959: A search party discovers the abandoned tent and the first remains of the group.
- March 1959: Soviet authorities conclude their official investigation into the disappearance.

## Factual Claims

- The location is Kholat Syakhl (Mountain of the Dead), a remote, high-altitude pass in the Ural Mountains.
- The tent was found with several cuts on the exterior fabric, which forensic analysis suggests were made from the inside.
- Several victims were discovered partially dressed (some in underwear or socks), indicating an immediate and desperate flight into sub-zero temperatures.
- Medical reports confirmed internal injuries, including fractured ribs and skull fractures, in some victims without corresponding external bruising.
- Footprints leading away from the tent showed evidence of rapid movement, with varying sizes and types appearing to indicate different pairs of shoes or barefooted individuals.
- Initial Soviet records reported 'anomalous' radiation levels on certain items found within the camp (notably personal belongings).
- The area is characterized by extreme weather patterns and topographical features capable of producing infrasound under specific wind conditions.

## Unanswered Questions

- The specific chemical or radioactive source for the 'anomalous radiation' reported in 1959.
- The physiological cause for internal trauma without external bruising (impact vs. pressure).
- The motive behind cutting the tent from the inside rather than simply unzipping it.
- The reason for the discrepancy in footwear and footprints found leading away from the site.
