# Brand Style Guide: Raat3Baje

## Essence (read first)
Face-led INDIAN horror investigation and folklore channel (evolved from the Case13B concept). The creator is the recognisable on-camera host. "Khayal3Baje tells animated horror stories. Raat3Baje investigates the story behind the story." It must never become a face-camera Khayal3Baje.
- **Universe:** Indian regional horror folklore; rare and obscure entities (Munja, Hadal, Jakhin, Nishi Daak, Chhalawa, Palta Hua Pair / reversed-foot bride, Pishach, Masaan, Brahmarakshasa, Vetala, Dayan/Chudail traditions); alleged encounters; origins of common Indian ghost stories; village beliefs; urban legends; highway ghost stories; Peepal-tree folklore; being called by name at night; 3 AM mythology; different regional versions of one legend; rational explanations where relevant.
- **Also:** some real/alleged and some clearly FICTIONAL but gripping ghost stories, "rules" horror (e.g. rules for a night shift, a haveli, a highway dhaba), REAL horror encounters told as stories (first-hand accounts people have published), and BLACK MAGIC (kala jadu, totke) and TANTRA stories (a tantrik, a shamshan sadhna, an aghori, a siddhi with a price). Fiction is always ORIGINAL -- inspired by folklore and research, never copied or closely paraphrased from any existing story, and presented as a story, not as fact.
- **Integrity:** clearly separate documented facts / folklore / oral tradition / alleged eyewitness accounts / later retellings / speculation / dramatisation. Never invent newspaper articles, records, dates, villages, witnesses or evidence to sound authentic.
- **Language/tone:** Hinglish in Roman script; atmospheric, investigative, direct eye contact.
- **Priority:** obscure Indian regional folklore and strong stories over the most overused internet horror subjects.
- **Length:** 12-20 min longs (a story episode runs longer if its story needs it -- see below). Shorts: 45-120 s -- one entity, one incident, one rule-set or one chilling detail.

## Formats
- **A -- Direct Investigation:** host talks to the audience; documentary/commentary mixing storytelling, research, folklore and analysis.
- **B -- Two-Person / Case13B:** the host plays two characters. Character 1 asks, challenges, voices what the viewer thinks; Character 2 has researched it and explains gradually. Must feel like a natural conversation, not an artificial interview.
- **C -- Case Reconstruction:** open on an alleged incident with a specific place, date or person WHEN reliable sourcing exists; reconstruct it, then investigate what can actually be established.

## Episode structure
Cold open / disturbing incident -> central mystery -> story or alleged encounter -> historical/folklore background -> different regional versions -> evidence or available documentation -> possible explanations -> second incident/variant where useful -> what can actually be established -> strong unresolved or thought-provoking ending.

## Story episodes (long videos): five formats in turn
Long-form story episodes take turns between five formats: **"Rules" horror** -> **a real horror encounter, told as a story** -> **a fictional horror story** -> **a black magic (kala jadu) story** -> **a tantra story**, and round again (the creator can pick the next one). All five are PURE STORY from the first second to the last:
- **No investigation, no research segment, no analysis.** No "iske peeche ka folklore", no regional versions, no cultural or psychological explanation, no fact/folklore breakdown, no "kya ye sach hai?", no host stepping out of the story to comment. Research only informs the world-building silently; it never appears in the script.
- The host is the narrator/storyteller, telling the story straight to camera (Format A as storytelling, not documentary). Formats B and C and the episode structure above do not apply to story episodes.
- Close on the story's own final line. At most a one-line outro (a CTA to comment or subscribe) -- never a recap or an explanation of what it meant.

## "Rules" horror in a long video
**Never open the video on the rules.** The viewer must know who the character is, how they got there and who handed them the rules before the first rule is read. The whole video is the story; it runs as long as it needs; never cut or rush rules to save time -- extend the video instead. Order:
1. **Cold open (20-40 s):** one unsettling moment from later in the story, or the character's situation -- not a rule.
2. **Setup:** who the character is (name, age, family, a detail of their life), what is going wrong for them, and why they take this job / stay in this place -- debt, a transfer, a sick parent, a missed train, a cheap hostel seat. Let us feel their need so we understand why they don't just leave.
3. **Arrival:** the place itself -- what they see, hear and smell on the first evening; what the locals say or refuse to say.
4. **The rule-giver:** a specific person -- the old chowkidar, the outgoing night-shift guy, the warden, the dhaba owner's mother -- with something odd about them (won't look at the door, leaves before sunset, hands shake, missing a finger). Show HOW the rules are given: a folded chit, a register, a list scratched on the wall, said once and never repeated. A short scene of dialogue between them.
5. **The rules -- as many as the story needs, often 8-12 or more.** Each rule gets its own moment: read slowly, then the character's reaction, what it seems to imply, a comment or silence from the rule-giver. Rules escalate from odd to terrifying; some can seem to contradict each other; one can be crossed out or missing.
6. **The question:** the character asks WHY these rules exist. The rule-giver deflects, gets angry, or gives half a reason that makes it worse ("Pichhle wale ne bhi yahi poocha tha."). Plant hints about the reason; never explain it fully here.
7. **The nights:** rules followed; small signs they are real; rules that start to make sense one by one.
8. **The break:** the night a rule is broken or nearly broken, and why (tiredness, doubt, someone calling their name, trying to save someone).
9. **The ending:** the twist -- the reason behind the rules, the rule nobody explained, or the truth about the rule-giver -- on an unresolved line. The video ends here: no investigation or explanation afterwards.

## Fictional horror story in a long video
An ORIGINAL Indian horror story (a haunted house, a village curse, a highway encounter, a possession, a cursed object, a wrong turn at night...), told as one continuous narrative. No rule list -- that is the other format. Order:
1. **Cold open (20-40 s):** one unsettling moment from later in the story.
2. **Setup:** the character (name, age, family, a detail of their life), what is going wrong for them and why they end up where the story happens.
3. **The place and the first wrong thing:** what they see, hear and smell; what the locals say or refuse to say; a small sign that something is off.
4. **Escalation:** three or four incidents, each worse than the last, each with its own scene, dialogue and sensory detail; the character's doubts, attempts to explain it away, someone who won't believe them.
5. **The discovery:** what is really going on, found out piece by piece inside the story (a diary, an old woman, a photograph) -- never by the narrator lecturing.
6. **The climax:** the confrontation or the escape attempt.
7. **The ending:** a twist on an unresolved, chilling final line.

## Real horror encounters, told as a story (long video)
A real, first-hand experience that someone has published -- found, read and cited by the researcher (a news report or interview, a book of collected accounts, a Reddit / Quora / forum post by the person it happened to) -- told as one continuous story in the creator's own storytelling voice.
- **The reported core stays as reported:** where, roughly when, who it happened to (by role -- "ek truck driver", "Pune ki ek nurse"; names changed or left out unless the person published them), what they saw and heard, in what order, and how it ended. Scene detail (the night, the room, the weather, small thoughts) may be filled in to tell it; nothing that changes what happened. Never invent a newspaper, a police record, a date or a witness to make it sound more real.
- **One opening line, inside the mood,** says whose experience this is and where it was shared ("Ye experience ek truck driver ka hai, jo usne kuch saal pehle ek forum par likha tha...") -- then straight into the story. No investigation afterwards, no "kya ye sach tha?", no explanation.
- **Point of view** follows the creator's writing style: when he tells stories as "main", he tells it as the person it happened to, and the opening line has said it is their experience.
- **Structure:** cold open (one moment from later) -> who they are and why they were there that night -> the place and the first wrong thing -> the incidents in the order reported, each worse -> the climax as reported -> what happened to them afterwards (if reported) -> the final, unresolved line.
- **Several accounts:** an episode may chain two or three real encounters on one theme (the same highway, the same entity, the same kind of night), each a complete story with its own opening line, the strongest last.

## Black magic and tantra stories (long video)
ORIGINAL stories, told as stories, in the world of Indian black magic and tantra: **kala jadu and totke** (a lemon and chillies at the door, a doll stuck with needles, hair or nails taken for a ritual, cursed food or water, a cursed object, a jealous relative's ritual, a possession a ritual started) and **tantra** (a tantrik's bargain, a shamshan sadhna at night, an aghori, a siddhi that demands a price, a masaan or pishach sadhna, a ritual that must not be interrupted).
- **The research decides the world:** how these beliefs really go -- the signs people look for, the remedies, the days and hours (amavasya, the cremation ground at midnight), the places, the words, who is said to do it and why. The story uses those real details so it feels true.
- **Respect:** Tantra is a living spiritual tradition and most of its followers are ordinary devotees. The horror comes from one person's misuse of power -- greed, jealousy, revenge -- never from mocking a religion, a community, a caste, a real temple or a living person. Never name a real person or family as a practitioner or a victim.
- **No real how-to:** a ritual is shown through what the character sees, hears and feels -- smoke, a half-heard chant, a circle of ash, a smell -- never as usable steps, real mantras, ingredients or quantities.
- **Structure:** cold open (one moment from later) -> who the character is and what they want or fear -> the first sign (something found at the door, a sudden illness, a smell, an animal behaving wrong) -> the explanation nobody believes, and the one person who knows -> escalation as the spell or the sadhna takes hold -> the attempt to undo it (a counter-ritual, a journey to someone who knows, a rule that must be kept) and its price -> the twist (who did it, why, or what the cure really cost) -> an unresolved final line.

## Recording session: the Shorts first, then the long video
Every long video is recorded in one sitting that opens with its 3-4 Shorts: the creator reads the Shorts one after another (a few seconds of silence after each), then says his greeting and records the long video. Each Short belongs to this episode: one complete moment, rule, real detail or incident from its world -- never the long story's twist -- with the hook in its first line and no greeting. The Studio splits the recording where the greeting is said.

## The face in the long video
Two layouts, chosen per channel or per project: **mixed** (his face comes back between the pictures all through) or **face first** (only his face for the first 1-2 minutes -- the greeting, the hook, why tonight's story matters, said straight to camera -- then pictures and clips only, to the end). For a face-first video the opening is written to be said to camera, and the story starts where the pictures take over.

## Production
The existing Raat3Baje/Case13B room is the primary set. Direct eye contact for narration. For two-character episodes separate the characters with framing, seating, lighting and performance -- no second set needed.

## For every video develop
Topic and central question; research plan and sources; fact/folklore distinction; 3-5 title options; thumbnail concept; opening hook; complete Hinglish script; choice of Direct / Two-Person / Reconstruction; character dialogue when applicable; A-roll instructions; camera positions; lighting/setup; scene-by-scene shot list; B-roll; images/maps/documents needed; editing instructions; sound design; retention devices; Shorts/Reels opportunities; connection to future Raat3Baje episodes.
For a story episode (any of the five story formats) drop the research plan, sources and fact/folklore distinction from that list; develop instead the story bible -- characters, place, the full rule list for a rules episode, the reported core of a real encounter, the beliefs a black magic or tantra story stands on, the twist -- the scene-by-scene beats, and the session's 3-4 Shorts.

## Edit and visuals (story style -- the creator's rule, 2026-10-02)
- Pictures and videos only. No subtitles/captions, headings, chapter titles, title cards, text on screen, newspaper or case-file cutaways, maps, charts, flashes, shakes or glitch hits. The render turns them off and strips them; do not plan them. The host's own face footage keeps a slow push-in on every take and a gentle punch-in about once a minute.
- No whooshes or sound effects. The music bed stays, far under the voice. The creator adds any sound edit by hand.
- About half of the visuals are moving clips (the creator can raise it to 100% per project). Clips go where something moves: a door easing open, a figure walking away, fog over the road.
- The visuals tell the story in order, like a storyboard: each shot is the next moment of what is being narrated, in the same place and the same night, until the story moves on. No generic, stock or news-looking pictures.
- Same characters in every shot. Each character has one fixed look (age, face, hair, clothes) from the project's cast sheet (`production/cast_sheet.json`), repeated word for word in every image and video prompt.
- Calm and scary, never rushed or news-like: slow steady movement, long quiet shots, darkness, mist and stillness carry the dread.
- No camera is ever in the picture. Image and video prompts describe only what the viewer sees, never the camera filming it: never write camera, DSLR, lens, tripod, photographer or film crew (the image model draws whatever is named, so "the camera pans across the corridor" puts a camera in the shot). Write the move itself: "slow pan across the corridor", "slow push toward the door". A camera appears only when it is part of the story (a CCTV camera on the wall).

## Music
Indian horror investigation: dark and grounded, never jump-scare cheese. Setting the scene and folklore: dark ambient, tanpura drone, low sarangi or shehnai, eerie/mysterious, 60-75 bpm. The investigation building: suspenseful, pulsing low strings, dholak or tabla kept sparse, 85-100 bpm. The reveal or the encounter: dramatic, taiko drums, aggressive strings. Just before a chilling line or a named entity: `off` -- silence is the scare. Never happy, bright or playful.

## Thumbnail (the creator's reference, 2026-10-21)

One fixed layout, made by BuzzEdit: the story's scene (AI picture, night, no
text) fills the frame; the creator's own base photo takes the right third
behind a torn white edge; 2-4 Devanagari words in blood red with drips sit
over the scene (the thing or place the story turns on); a two-word English
label (HORROR STORY, REAL ENCOUNTER ...) top-left. The base photo lives at
`B:/youtubeProjects/Raat3Baje/thumbnail_base.png` (or beside a recording as
`thumbnail_base.*`); replace that file to change his picture on every
thumbnail. Writers and the SEO manager supply the words and the scene, never
a layout.
