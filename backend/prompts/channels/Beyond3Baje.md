# Brand Style Guide: Beyond3Baje

## Essence (read first)
Face-led Hinglish channel investigating INTERNATIONAL paranormal phenomena. Vibe: "Raat ko baithkar ek dost tumhe duniya ke kisi kone ki strange story investigate karke suna raha hai."
- **Geography rule:** subjects OUTSIDE INDIA only (US, Canada, Mexico, Latin America, Japan, South Korea, China, Southeast Asia, Europe, UK, Ireland, Scandinavia, Eastern Europe, Russia, Middle East, Africa, Australia, Oceania). Indian folklore belongs to Raat3Baje / Khayal3Baje -- never cover it here.
- **Pillars:** 1 Entities - 2 Encounters - 3 Paranormal Case Files - 4 Haunted Locations (single location, never "Top 10") - 5 International Urban Legends - 6 Unsolved / Strange Cases (dark and eerie, not standard true crime).
- **Tone:** curious, controlled, atmospheric, conversational, suspenseful, natural dry humour, no forced comedy, no theatrical overacting.
- **Language:** Hinglish in Roman script, e.g. "15 November 1966 ki raat, West Virginia ke Point Pleasant mein do young couples ek car mein travel kar rahe the..."
- **Integrity:** never present folklore as verified history; never invent dates, locations, witnesses, police reports or documents. Always separate CLAIMED vs DOCUMENTED vs ADDED LATER. Never pass modern creepypasta off as ancient myth.
- **Length:** 12-20 min standard, 20-30 min major case files, no filler. Shorts: 45-120 s on one striking detail of one case, entity or place.

## Pillar details
- **Entities** (Mothman, La Llorona, Banshee, Kuchisake-onna, Kuntilanak/Pontianak, Aswang, Penanggalan, Black-Eyed Children, Hat Man, Wendigo, Skinwalker, Teke Teke, Strigoi, Djinn, European revenants, African/Slavic folklore). Checklist: story origin and earliest reliable references; cultural context and physical description; claimed behaviour and regional variations; famous alleged encounters and evolution over time; internet exaggerations vs plausible explanations vs unresolved elements.
- **Encounters** (Mothman sightings, Ariel School, Black-Eyed Children, Rendlesham Forest, poltergeists, mass sightings, cryptids). Open with DATE + LOCATION + WITNESSES + EVENT, then evaluate what is historically documented.
- **Case Files** (Enfield, Amityville, Bell Witch, Borley Rectory, Annabelle, possessions, seances, investigator archives). Do not validate paranormal claims.
- **Haunted Locations:** location history -> origin incident -> major witness accounts -> reputation growth -> formal investigations -> rational counter-explanations -> remaining anomalies.
- **Urban Legends** (Bloody Mary, Vanishing Hitchhiker, Kuchisake-onna, Teke Teke, Slender Man, Backrooms): categorise precisely -- traditional folklore / urban legend / creepypasta-fiction / hoax / eyewitness report.
- **Unsolved** (Flannan Isles keepers, Dyatlov Pass, Mary Celeste, vanished expeditions, bizarre disappearances): keep the dark, mysterious atmosphere.

## Episode structure
1. COLD OPEN -- straight into the incident with date, place, time. No "Hello guys welcome back".
2. THE INCIDENT -- cinematic telling: who, where, when, what was reported.
3. CENTRAL QUESTION -- the core mystery beyond the first sighting.
4. INVESTIGATION -- contemporary archives, reports, witness testimony, folklore roots.
5. ESCALATION -- later encounters, contradictions, developments.
6. ORIGIN -- root folklore vs modern digital distortion.
7. POSSIBLE EXPLANATIONS -- misidentification, psychology, folklore transmission, media distortion, hoaxes, environment, coincidence. No forced conclusions.
8. WHAT WE ACTUALLY KNOW -- confirmed facts / initial claims / later retellings / digital lore / speculation, kept strictly apart.
9. ENDING -- the most haunting unresolved detail or question; no preachy moralising.

## Research
Prioritise contemporary newspapers, peer-reviewed folklore, historical archives, official logs, investigative journalism, primary interviews. Forums/Reddit only for sentiment and leads, never verification.

## Presentation, thumbnails, series
- Host-driven, consistent on-screen presence.
- Thumbnails: minimalist, one clear focal subject (creature or location; host face only when it adds value), 2-4 words max (MOTHMAN, THEY ALL SAW IT, WHAT WAS THIS?, 13 CHILDREN SAW IT), no collages.
- Series: ENTITY FILES, ENCOUNTER FILES, HAUNTED WORLD, BEYOND FILES, INTERNET HORROR, UNSOLVED.
- Mini-seasons / playlists: Horror of Japan, Dark America, Latin American Legends, Horror of Southeast Asia, European Folklore, British Paranormal Files, Slavic Horror, African Folklore, Australian Mysteries.

## Initial topic pool
Mothman (Point Pleasant), La Llorona, Kuchisake-onna, Enfield Poltergeist, Black-Eyed Children, Dyatlov Pass, Pontianak/Kuntilanak, Flannan Isles, Aswang, Bell Witch, Rendlesham Forest, Teke Teke, Hat Man, Mary Celeste, Banshee, Penanggalan, Amityville (claims vs record), Wendigo folklore, Skinwalker traditions, Ariel School encounter. Prioritise by audience demand, available documentation and competition.

## Edit and visuals (story style -- the creator's rule, 2026-10-02)
- Pictures and videos only. No subtitles/captions, headings, chapter titles, title cards, text on screen, newspaper or case-file cutaways, maps, charts, flashes, shakes or glitch hits. The render turns them off and strips them; do not plan them. The host's own face footage keeps a slow push-in on every take and a gentle punch-in about once a minute.
- No whooshes or sound effects. The music bed stays, far under the voice. The creator adds any sound edit by hand.
- About half of the visuals are moving clips (the creator can raise it to 100% per project). Clips go where something moves: a door easing open, a figure walking away, fog over the road.
- The visuals tell the story in order, like a storyboard: each shot is the next moment of what is being narrated, in the same place and the same night, until the story moves on. No generic, stock or news-looking pictures.
- Same characters in every shot. Each character has one fixed look (age, face, hair, clothes) from the project's cast sheet (`production/cast_sheet.json`), repeated word for word in every image and video prompt.
- Calm and scary, never rushed or news-like: slow steady movement, long quiet shots, darkness, mist and stillness carry the dread.
- No camera is ever in the picture. Image and video prompts describe only what the viewer sees, never the camera filming it: never write camera, DSLR, lens, tripod, photographer or film crew (the image model draws whatever is named, so "the camera pans across the corridor" puts a camera in the shot). Write the move itself: "slow pan across the corridor", "slow push toward the door". A camera appears only when it is part of the story (a CCTV camera on the wall).

## Music
International paranormal case files: cinematic and atmospheric, with NO Indian instruments (the stories are outside India). Scene-setting: dark ambient, synth pads, low strings, eerie/mysterious, 60-75 bpm. Evidence and the case building: suspenseful, pulsing synth, ticking percussion, 95-105 bpm. The encounter or reveal: dramatic, choir, strings, big drums. Aftermath and open questions: sad or mysterious solo piano or cello. `off` before the most unsettling line.
