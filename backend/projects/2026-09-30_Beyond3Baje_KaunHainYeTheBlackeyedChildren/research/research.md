# Research package

## Timeline

- Pre-1970s: Emerging oral reports of 'strange children' or 'dark-eyed entities' in various rural and suburban communities.
- 1970s - 1980s: First wave of documented sightings in North America (USA and Canada), where witnesses reported children with solid black eyes appearing at doorsteps or near vehicles.
- Late 1980s: Initial appearance of the 'Black-Eyed Children' (BEK) narrative in regional news segments and community newsletters, primarily in Midwestern United States states.
- Early 2000s: The transition to digital platforms; reports began appearing on early paranormal forums and specialized urban legend websites.
- 2010 - Present: The 'Creepypasta' era. The story became a global internet phenomenon, where modern elements (specific supernatural powers, teleportation claims, and standardized 'rules') were added by online communities.

## Factual Claims

- Physical Description: Reported entities consistently have eyes that appear entirely black, with no visible iris or sclera.
- Common Behavior Patterns: Early reports from the 1980s/90s emphasize a specific behavior: children asking for permission to enter a home or car (e.g., 'Can we come in?' or 'Where is your mother?').
- Physiological Response: Many witnesses report an immediate, instinctive feeling of dread or a biological fear response before the interaction begins.
- Geographic Distribution: Documented reports prior to 2010 are concentrated in North America (USA, Canada), but similar accounts exist in Europe and Australia.
- Distinction between Report vs. Fiction: Historical reports focus on 'unsettling appearance' and 'strange requests,' while modern creepypasta adds elements like 'glitching,' supernatural abilities, and specific ritualistic behaviors.
