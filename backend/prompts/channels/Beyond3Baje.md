# Brand Style Guide: Beyond3Baje

## Essence (read first)
Face-led Hinglish channel investigating INTERNATIONAL paranormal phenomena. Vibe: "Raat ko baithkar ek dost tumhe duniya ke kisi kone ki strange story investigate karke suna raha hai."
- **Geography rule:** subjects OUTSIDE INDIA only (US, Canada, Mexico, Latin America, Japan, South Korea, China, Southeast Asia, Europe, UK, Ireland, Scandinavia, Eastern Europe, Russia, Middle East, Africa, Australia, Oceania). Indian folk horror (village ghosts, chudail, black magic) belongs to Raat3Baje / Khayal3Baje -- never cover it here. **Exception (creator, 2026-11-13): Indian mythology is a Beyond3Baje pillar** -- the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Puranas and their people and places (Sita, Ram, Hanuman, Ravan, Draupadi, Karna, Ayodhya, Lanka, Kurukshetra...).
- **Pillars:** 1 Entities - 2 Encounters - 3 Paranormal Case Files - 4 Haunted Locations (single location, never "Top 10") - 5 International Urban Legends - 6 Unsolved / Strange Cases (dark and eerie, not standard true crime) - 7 Indian Mythology (MYTHOLOGY FILES: the epics retold and examined, realistic and cinematic).
- **Tone:** curious, controlled, atmospheric, conversational, suspenseful, natural dry humour, no forced comedy, no theatrical overacting.
- **Language:** Hinglish in Roman script, e.g. "15 November 1966 ki raat, West Virginia ke Point Pleasant mein do young couples ek car mein travel kar rahe the..."
- **Integrity:** never present folklore as verified history; never invent dates, locations, witnesses, police reports or documents. Always separate CLAIMED vs DOCUMENTED vs ADDED LATER. Never pass modern creepypasta off as ancient myth.
- **Length:** 12-20 min standard, 20-30 min major case files, no filler. Shorts: 45-120 s on one striking detail of one case, entity or place.

## Pillar details
- **Entities** (Mothman, La Llorona, Banshee, Kuchisake-onna, Kuntilanak/Pontianak, Aswang, Penanggalan, Black-Eyed Children, Hat Man, Wendigo, Skinwalker, Teke Teke, Strigoi, Djinn, European revenants, African/Slavic folklore). Checklist: story origin and earliest reliable references; cultural context and physical description; claimed behaviour and regional variations; famous alleged encounters and evolution over time; internet exaggerations vs plausible explanations vs unresolved elements.
- **Encounters** (Mothman sightings, Ariel School, Black-Eyed Children, Rendlesham Forest, poltergeists, mass sightings, cryptids). Open with DATE + LOCATION + WITNESSES + EVENT, then evaluate what is historically documented.
- **Case Files** (Enfield, Amityville, Bell Witch, Borley Rectory, Annabelle, possessions, seances, investigator archives). Do not validate paranormal claims.
- **Haunted Locations:** location history -> origin incident -> major witness accounts -> reputation growth -> formal investigations -> rational counter-explanations -> remaining anomalies.
- **Urban Legends** (Bloody Mary, Vanishing Hitchhiker, Kuchisake-onna, Teke Teke, Slender Man, Backrooms): categorise precisely -- traditional folklore / urban legend / creepypasta-fiction / hoax / eyewitness report.
- **Unsolved** (Flannan Isles keepers, Dyatlov Pass, Mary Celeste, vanished expeditions, bizarre disappearances): keep the dark, mysterious atmosphere.
- **Indian Mythology** (Sita, Ram, Hanuman, Ravan, Draupadi, Karna, Bhishma, Lanka, Ayodhya, Panchavati, Kurukshetra, the Puranic lore). Retell the episode cinematically, then examine it: what the source text says (Valmiki Ramayana, Vyasa's Mahabharata, the Puranas), what regional and later versions added (Tulsidas, Kamban, folk retellings, TV serials), and what is popular belief. Name the version you quote. Respectful, never mocking faith; questions are asked, not sneered. Places that exist today (Ayodhya, Rameswaram, Ram Setu, Sri Lanka sites) are named as believed or traditional sites, never as proven.

## Episode structure
1. COLD OPEN -- straight into the incident with date, place, time. No "Hello guys welcome back".
2. THE INCIDENT -- cinematic telling: who, where, when, what was reported.
3. CENTRAL QUESTION -- the core mystery beyond the first sighting.
4. INVESTIGATION -- contemporary archives, reports, witness testimony, folklore roots.
5. ESCALATION -- later encounters, contradictions, developments.
6. ORIGIN -- root folklore vs modern digital distortion.
7. POSSIBLE EXPLANATIONS -- misidentification, psychology, folklore transmission, media distortion, hoaxes, environment, coincidence. No forced conclusions.
8. WHAT WE ACTUALLY KNOW -- confirmed facts / initial claims / later retellings / digital lore / speculation, kept strictly apart.
9. ENDING -- the most haunting unresolved detail or question; no preachy moralising.

## Recording session: the Shorts first, then the long video
Every long video is recorded in one sitting that opens with its 3-4 Shorts: the creator reads them one after another (a few seconds of silence after each), then says his greeting and records the long video. Each Short belongs to this episode: one striking detail of the case, entity or place (the encounter with DATE + PLACE + WHO when documented, one witness's claim, one contradiction, the strangest unexplained detail), complete in 45-75 seconds, hook first, no greeting, never the long video's final answer. The Studio splits the recording where the greeting is said.

## The face in the long video
Two layouts, chosen per channel or per project: **mixed** (his face comes back between the pictures all through) or **face first** (only his face for the first 1-2 minutes -- the greeting, the cold open and the central question, said straight to camera -- then pictures and clips only). For a face-first video the opening is written to be said to camera.

## Research
Prioritise contemporary newspapers, peer-reviewed folklore, historical archives, official logs, investigative journalism, primary interviews. Forums/Reddit only for sentiment and leads, never verification.

## Presentation, thumbnails, series
- Host-driven, consistent on-screen presence.
- Thumbnails: minimalist, one clear focal subject (creature or location; host face only when it adds value), 2-4 words max (MOTHMAN, THEY ALL SAW IT, WHAT WAS THIS?, 13 CHILDREN SAW IT), no collages.
- Series: ENTITY FILES, ENCOUNTER FILES, HAUNTED WORLD, BEYOND FILES, INTERNET HORROR, UNSOLVED.
- Mini-seasons / playlists: Horror of Japan, Dark America, Latin American Legends, Horror of Southeast Asia, European Folklore, British Paranormal Files, Slavic Horror, African Folklore, Australian Mysteries.

## What If stories (the creator's direction, 2026-10-23)
Alongside the investigations, Beyond3Baje tells **"What If this happened to
you?"** stories: second-person ("Aap") Hinglish psychological horror in which
ONE impossible rule enters an everyday Indian night (a 2 BHK flat, an ancestral
village house, a night highway, a train compartment) and defeats the viewer's
own logic (the phone camera, the building CCTV, a WhatsApp call, a hotel room).
The uncanny mechanic may come from anywhere in the world (this is the one
format where the geography rule does not apply to the *setting*); the viewer's
home is where it happens. Uncanny detail, never gore; proximity escalating from
outside to behind you; a written ending decided before scene one; a gut-punch
final line with nothing after it. Presented as a story, never as an alleged real
incident. The `WhatIfWriter` persona holds the blueprint (six beats for a
75-second Short; scenes with `[SFX]`/`[VISUAL]` cues for 5-25 minutes). The
length is always a setting: the Short's length pick, or the project's target
runtime. Sixty-odd premises are in the Topic Vault (`format: what_if`), five
marked as pilots. The series title: **WHAT IF**.

## The Anomaly Files (SCP-style, 2026-10-23)
A sister series: **ORIGINAL** anomalies in India told as containment reports
read aloud (item `B3B-###`, class, containment procedures, description,
discovery log, incident logs, one redaction that is the twist). The SCP wiki
inspires the *form* only; none of its entries, numbers, names or text is ever
used, and the file is presented as fiction. Same writer persona, same length
rule. Series title: **ANOMALY FILES**.

## Initial topic pool
Mothman (Point Pleasant), La Llorona, Kuchisake-onna, Enfield Poltergeist, Black-Eyed Children, Dyatlov Pass, Pontianak/Kuntilanak, Flannan Isles, Aswang, Bell Witch, Rendlesham Forest, Teke Teke, Hat Man, Mary Celeste, Banshee, Penanggalan, Amityville (claims vs record), Wendigo folklore, Skinwalker traditions, Ariel School encounter. Prioritise by audience demand, available documentation and competition.

## Edit and visuals (story style -- the creator's rule, 2026-10-02)
- Pictures and videos only. No subtitles/captions, headings, chapter titles, title cards, text on screen, newspaper or case-file cutaways, maps, charts, flashes, shakes or glitch hits. The render turns them off and strips them; do not plan them. The host's own face footage keeps a slow push-in on every take and a gentle punch-in about once a minute.
- No whooshes or sound effects. The music bed stays, far under the voice. The creator adds any sound edit by hand.
- About half of the visuals are moving clips (the creator can raise it to 100% per project). Clips go where something moves: a door easing open, a figure walking away, fog over the road.
- The visuals tell the story in order, like a storyboard: each shot is the next moment of what is being narrated, in the same place and the same night, until the story moves on. No generic, stock or news-looking pictures.
- Same characters in every shot. Each character has one fixed look (age, face, hair, clothes) from the project's cast sheet (`production/cast_sheet.json`), repeated word for word in every image and video prompt.
- Calm and scary, never rushed or news-like: slow steady movement, long quiet shots, darkness, mist and stillness carry the dread.
- No camera is ever in the picture. Image and video prompts describe only what the viewer sees, never the camera filming it: never write camera, DSLR, lens, tripod, photographer or film crew (the image model draws whatever is named, so "the camera pans across the corridor" puts a camera in the shot). Write the move itself: "slow pan across the corridor", "slow push toward the door". A camera appears only when it is part of the story (a CCTV camera on the wall).

## Art style (the creator's choice per story, 2026-10-21)
A Beyond3Baje story is made in one of five media, picked in the Topic Vault
(per story), on the Projects page (per project) or in Settings -> Channel
Profiles (the channel's default). The step prompt names the project's style;
every picture, clip, thumbnail scene and text card follows it, and the
PromptEngineer, CharacterPlanner and ThumbnailSpecialist write for it:

- **Real** (default): cinematic photoreal pictures and clips, the rules above.
- **Explainer**: flat vector explainer illustrations -- cutaways, maps with a
  dotted drift line, simple figures beside a scale bar. Numbers, distances and
  durations are the point: short `popup` / `stat_callout` / `definition_card`
  text next to the picture, never drawn inside it.
- **Stick figure**: black marker stick figures on white paper, one red accent,
  one idea per frame; short hand-written labels beside the drawing.
- **Cartoon**: bold 2D cartoon frames, thick outlines, flat cel shading,
  bright colours, expressive characters; pictures only, no text.
- **Stick world** (2026-10-29, default for What If, Anomaly Files, Case Files
  and Unsolved): dressed stick figures standing in a hand-painted illustrated
  world of their period -- any era, prehistoric to the future -- instead of a
  photoreal recreation of a claimed event. A plain picture is the PLACE itself
  (rich period detail, nobody close up); the witness, the entity or the
  containment-report moment is a `stick_scene` beat dressed for its `era`, or
  a named character's own fixed `outfit` from the cast sheet. Good wherever a
  real-looking recreation would overclaim; maps/timelines/charts still carry
  the case's own facts.

In a drawn style nobody gets a real face (no face swap), the camera rule still
holds, and the story-order storyboard rule still holds.

## Mythology episodes: the look (creator, 2026-11-13)
Realistic mythological, never cartoon, never TV-serial plastic: cinematic photoreal
scenes of the epic age -- forests, ashrams, palaces of carved stone, rivers, oil
lamps and fire light, period clothing and ornaments, warm golden light or dusk,
mist and dust, a painterly epic grandeur like a big-budget historical film. The
gods and heroes look human and dignified (no blue plastic skin, no glowing halos
unless the text describes divine light). Maps, when a journey or a place needs
one, are aged hand-painted maps on parchment or palm leaf with the epic's place
names (Ayodhya, Chitrakoot, Panchavati, Kishkindha, Lanka) and a route drawn in
ink -- made as a picture, never a modern flat map.

## Music
International paranormal case files: cinematic and atmospheric, with NO Indian instruments (the stories are outside India). Scene-setting: dark ambient, synth pads, low strings, eerie/mysterious, 60-75 bpm. Evidence and the case building: suspenseful, pulsing synth, ticking percussion, 95-105 bpm. The encounter or reveal: dramatic, choir, strings, big drums. Aftermath and open questions: sad or mysterious solo piano or cello. `off` before the most unsettling line.
Mythology episodes are the exception: Indian instruments belong there -- bansuri, tanpura drone, sitar or veena, temple bells, conch, deep male chant or choir, big dhol/pakhawaj for battles; devotional calm for the quiet moments, epic and grand for the war and the reveal.
