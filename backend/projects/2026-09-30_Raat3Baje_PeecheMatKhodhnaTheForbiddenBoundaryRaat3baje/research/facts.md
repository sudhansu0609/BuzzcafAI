# Factual Claims

- Gram Devtas (village deities) are a widely documented feature of rural Indian religious practice, functioning as localized protector spirits of village boundaries, land, and community welfare, distinct from pan-Hindu deities.
- Pishach and Pret are recurring figures in Hindu mythology and rural oral tradition: Pishach are typically described as flesh-eating, non-human entities associated with unclean or unconsecrated ground (including cremation grounds/Masaan), while Pret refers to restless spirits of the dead whose last rites were incomplete or who died violent/untimely deaths.
- Masaan (cremation ground) is a recurring 'forbidden ground' concept in Indian folklore, treated across regions as spiritually dangerous or liminal space requiring ritual caution.
- Sacred groves (known regionally as Kavu in Kerala and by other names elsewhere in India) are a documented ecological-cum-spiritual tradition in southern India, where patches of forest/land are left undisturbed due to belief in protector spirits or deities residing there.
- Tantric and folk-ritual concepts of 'sealing' or protecting land/objects (referenced in oral tradition as Raksha Kavach-type protective rites) exist across Indian folk-religious practice, typically involving mantras, yantras, or ritual offerings performed before construction or land use on long-neglected ground.
