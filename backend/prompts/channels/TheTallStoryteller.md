# Brand Style Guide: TheTallStoryteller

## Essence (read first)
A storytelling channel of detective, comedy and crime stories, narrated start to finish in the creator's
own cloned voice (`sudhansu_story`) -- no face, no recording session, no live
set. The viewer is listening to one voice tell a good story, in Hinglish, the
way he would tell it to a friend late at night. Default length: a long story is
15-25 minutes (20 by default); a Short/reel is one minute (60 seconds).
- **Genres (creator, 2026-10-24):** mostly **detective** and **comedy**, sometimes
  **crime**. **No horror, ever** -- no ghosts, curses, black magic or haunted
  places as the story, no horror tone or music (Raat3Baje and Beyond3Baje are
  where horror lives). A detective case may *expose* a fake haunting, told
  as a puzzle with a human answer, never as a scare.
- **Language/tone:** Hinglish in Roman script; on-screen text (titles, captions
  if any) is also Hinglish. Warm, unhurried, a little mischievous when the
  story allows it -- never a lecture, never a listicle.
- **Voice and visuals:** narrated only, `art_style: stick_world` by default
  (2026-10-29 -- see "Art style" below; a project may still switch to real,
  explainer, stick figure or cartoon on the Projects page), no face on screen
  at all (synthetic take: `face_layout: face_intro` at 0 seconds unless the
  creator sets his own face-intro clip for this channel on the Story Desk).
- **Rights:** public-domain classics (anything whose author died before 1966,
  `story_library.rights_for`) may be named and retold directly. A living or
  still-protected author's work is never retold as theirs -- only as an
  original story "inspired by" its premise, the way Raat3Baje's fiction is
  inspired by folklore and never copied.

## Detective stories (the heart of the channel)
A detective episode is a crime or a puzzle, a sharp
detective, clues laid fairly in front of the listener, a misdirection, and a
reveal that makes the listener replay the clues in his head. Bengali detective
fiction is the heart of it (the Kolkata adda, the para, the old mansion, the
zamindar's will, the trams and the gaslit lanes), with the world's classics
retold in Indian settings.
- **Public domain, retold directly:** Sherlock Holmes (Conan Doyle, d. 1930),
  Father Brown (Chesterton, d. 1936), Auguste Dupin (Poe, d. 1849), Arsene Lupin
  (Leblanc, d. 1941), Bengal's first detective writers -- Priyanath
  Mukhopadhyay's police memoirs *Darogar Daptar* (d. 1947), Panchkari Dey (d. 1945),
  Dinendra Kumar Roy's Robert Blake stories (d. 1943). A retelling may move the
  case to Kolkata or another Indian city and give the detective an Indian name.
- **Protected, inspiration only:** Byomkesh Bakshi (Saradindu Bandyopadhyay,
  d. 1970), Feluda (Satyajit Ray, d. 1992), Kiriti Roy (Nihar Ranjan Gupta),
  Parashar Barma (Premendra Mitra), Ibn-e-Safi's Imran and Faridi -- their
  characters, names, cases and plots are theirs and are never used. What may be
  borrowed is the feel: an Indian thinking detective, a loyal narrator friend,
  a city with secrets. The channel's own recurring detective is an original
  character (name and quirks set in the first episode, kept in every one after).
- **Every episode:** the detective appears early; three to five clues, each
  said plainly once; one red herring; the reveal explains every clue; no
  supernatural solution (a ghost may be suspected, never the answer).

## Comedy stories
An original, light story that escalates a misunderstanding, a lie, a scheme
or a small disaster to a satisfying, funny payoff: an Indian everyday world
(a wedding, a mohalla, an office, a train, a joint family), characters who
want something badly, each beat funnier than the last, the punchline planted
early. The jokes come from character and situation, never from insults,
caste, religion or body-shaming. The recurring detective may star in a comic
case.

## Crime stories (sometimes)
A grounded crime told as a story -- a heist, a con, a cover-up, a chase --
from the inside, with a turn the listener did not see coming. Human motives,
human answers; no gore and no how-to for a real crime.

## Structure of a good story episode
Every genre on this channel follows the same shape, scaled to the episode's
length:
1. **Hook (10-30 s):** one line or image from inside the story -- a question,
   a threat, a wish, a joke that lands -- never "Hello doston" first. The
   greeting comes right after, folded into the opening, not before it.
2. **Setup:** who this is about, what they want or fear, and the world they
   are in -- just enough to care, in a sentence or two per character, not a
   biography.
3. **Rising stakes:** the story's own genre decides the engine -- a
   detective case's clues, a comedy's escalating misunderstanding, a crime's
   tightening net. Each beat is worse, funnier or stranger than the last;
   nothing repeats.
4. **Turn:** the moment that changes what the story is about -- a reveal, a
   reversal, a punchline, the twist a case or a crime earns.
   Planted earlier, never pulled from nowhere.
5. **Payoff:** the ending the genre promises -- the reveal that explains
   every clue for a detective story; the last, biggest laugh for a comedy; a
   final image or line for a crime story, with nothing explained afterwards. Close
   on the story's own last line; at most one short line after it (a CTA),
   never a recap of "what it meant."

## Pacing for narration
Written to be heard once, not read: short sentences, one idea per sentence,
concrete nouns over abstractions. Mark the places the voice should breathe --
`(pause)` after a reveal or a punchline, a slower passage before a turn.
Dialogue is rare and brief (this is told, not performed): a line or two of
real speech lands harder than a page of exchange. A comedy's rhythm needs its
beats landing at the right distance -- do not rush the setup of a joke to save
time; a thriller's clock needs short sentences and short scenes as it tightens.

## What to avoid
- No captions, headings, chapter-title cards, text effects, newspaper or
  case-file cutaways, maps, charts, flashes, shakes or glitch hits -- see
  "Edit and visuals" below.
- No lecturing asides, no "yeh sach hai ya nahi" investigation breaks, no
  moral stated outright -- the story carries its own meaning.
- No borrowing a protected author's text, characters or exact plot beats
  verbatim; a classic named as itself must actually be public domain.
- Never open on the hook and then stall -- the second line should already be
  moving the story forward.
- No horror: no ghost, curse, possession or black magic as the story, no
  horror tone, no creepy music. A suspected ghost in a detective case is a
  clue to a human trick.

## Shorts / reels (one minute, 60 seconds)
A Short is one complete story beat, never a trailer for the long one: the
funniest line of a comedy, one clue and its reveal from a detective case,
the turn of a crime told as its own tiny crime story. Hook in the first line, no greeting,
no "part 1 of 10" -- complete in itself even when it shares a world with a
long story. One format per idea: `detective`, `comedy` or `crime`.

## Edit and visuals (story style, same rule as Raat3Baje/Khayal3Baje/Beyond3Baje)
- Pictures and videos only. No subtitles/captions, headings, chapter titles,
  title cards, text on screen, newspaper or case-file cutaways, maps, charts,
  zoom punches, flashes, shakes or glitch hits. The render turns them off and
  strips them; do not plan them.
- No whooshes or transition sound effects. The music bed stays, well under the
  voice. The scene's own sounds (footsteps, rain, a door, a crowd) stay on.
- About half of the visuals are moving clips (the creator can raise it to
  100% per project) -- same `visual_style: story` rule the other story
  channels use, chosen here because the channel is narrated world-building
  exactly like them.
- The visuals tell the story in order, like a storyboard: each shot is the
  next moment of what is being narrated, until the story moves on. No
  generic, stock or news-looking pictures.
- Same characters in every shot: each character keeps one fixed look (age,
  face, hair, clothes) from the project's cast sheet (`production/cast_sheet.json`),
  repeated word for word in every image and video prompt.
- No camera is ever in the picture: never write camera, DSLR, lens, tripod,
  photographer or film crew in a prompt -- describe only what the viewer sees.
- There is no face on this channel (narrated, no recording session): nobody
  is marked `face=main` unless the creator sets his own face-intro clip for
  it.

## Art style (2026-10-29: stick world by default)
Every format on this channel defaults to **Stick world**: dressed stick
figures standing in a hand-painted illustrated world of their own period --
prehistoric to the future, whichever era the story names -- instead of a
photoreal recreation of people who were never recorded. A plain picture is
the PLACE itself (rich period detail, nobody close up); the detective, the
witness, a comic misunderstanding or a crime's turn is a `stick_scene` beat
dressed for its `era`, or a named character's own fixed `outfit` from the
cast sheet, reused every time they are on screen. Unlike the plain story
rule above, short on-screen text and `map`/`timeline`/`chart` beats ARE part
of this medium -- a detective's clue board or a case's dates may use them. A
project may still be switched to real, explainer, stick figure or cartoon on
the Projects page; the camera rule and the story-order storyboard rule hold
in every medium.

## Music
Playful, light percussion and pizzicato for comedy; low strings, a ticking
pulse and a curious piano for a detective case; a tight, driving pulse for a
crime story. Never horror_dark or creepy cues on this channel. `off` before a punchline, a reveal or the final line -- let
the silence land it.
